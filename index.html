Decisions in health systems, and the data behind them

We build methods and open data resources for the decisions that shape health care — how hospitals are paid, how care is scheduled and allocated, and how outbreaks are tracked. Where the underlying data is public but hard to use, we publish it in a form other people can actually work with.

U.S. Measles Tracker

County-level measles surveillance for the United States, run by Johns Hopkins and updated weekly. What we work on

The methodological core is inverse optimization — recovering what a decision maker was actually trying to achieve from the decisions they were observed to make, and establishing when a model learned that way can be trusted. Applications run from surgical scheduling and treatment planning to hospital payment policy and surveillance-driven resource allocation.

Alongside the methods work, the lab treats public data infrastructure as a research output in its own right. Health systems publish a great deal that is technically open and practically unusable; turning that into documented, citable datasets is part of what we do.
