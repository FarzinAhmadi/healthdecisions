U.S. Measles Tracker

County-level measles surveillance for the United States. State and county health departments report measles cases on their own schedules and in their own formats; the tracker turns that into a single, timely national picture at the geographic resolution outbreaks actually happen. It is updated weekly on Fridays.

The tracker is a Johns Hopkins project, run by the Center for Systems Science and Engineering and the International Vaccine Access Center. This page is my own summary of it. The tracker itself lives at IVAC ↗.

Why county-level matters

National and state case counts are what get reported, and they hide the thing that drives transmission. Measles moves through local pockets of low immunization, and a state number can look unremarkable while a handful of counties inside it are at serious risk. Tracking at county level makes that visible early enough to matter.

The tracker is built and maintained by Johns Hopkins University. It is led by William Moss, Lauren Gardner and Shaun Truelove, and brings together the International Vaccine Access Center, the Center for Systems Science and Engineering, and the Bloomberg Center for Government Excellence. I built the county-level data system as a member of the CSSE team, and I am first author on the 2025 JAMA research letter describing it. The tracker is not a Towson University product and nothing on this page should be read as speaking for the Johns Hopkins team.

Related work

The lab's current work in this area extends the tracker toward school-level immunization coverage. Every state publishes school-level data and every state defines it differently — different grade groupings, exemption categories and reporting thresholds — so no usable national picture exists. Building one is an ongoing project with undergraduate researchers at Towson.
